# Order Cancellation Policy

## Document Metadata

Document Owner: Order Management  
Document Type: Enterprise Policy  
Version: 1.0  
Status: Active  
Last Reviewed: October 2026  

---

## 1. Purpose

This policy defines when customer orders may be cancelled, how cancellation requests should be handled, and when cancellation requests must transition into return or fulfillment workflows.

The objective is to ensure that cancellations are processed consistently without creating inventory, payment, or fulfillment discrepancies.

---

## 2. Standard Cancellation Eligibility

Customers may request cancellation while an order remains in a cancellable state.

An order is generally considered cancellable when it:

- has been successfully created,
- has not yet been shipped,
- has not entered a locked fulfillment stage,
- has not already been completed or returned.

Cancellation is not guaranteed merely because a request was submitted before shipment.

Operational conditions may prevent cancellation after fulfillment preparation has begun.

---

## 3. Processing Orders

Orders with a status of Processing may still be eligible for cancellation.

Eligibility depends on whether the order has already been:

- allocated to inventory,
- assigned to a fulfillment facility,
- packed,
- transferred to the carrier.

If the order has not reached a non-reversible fulfillment stage, Customer Operations may attempt cancellation.

---

## 4. Shipped Orders

An order that has already been shipped should not normally be processed as a standard cancellation.

If a customer no longer wants an order after shipment:

1. the shipment should normally continue to delivery,
2. the customer should follow the standard return process,
3. eligibility for refund should be determined under the Return and Refund Policy.

Exceptions may apply when a carrier-intercept capability is available.

---

## 5. Completed Orders

Completed orders are not eligible for standard cancellation.

Requests involving completed orders should be evaluated under the Return and Refund Policy.

A completed order should not be changed to Cancelled solely because the customer later requests a refund.

---

## 6. Cancellation Reasons

Cancellation reasons should be captured whenever possible.

Common reason categories may include:

- customer changed mind,
- incorrect product selected,
- duplicate order,
- payment issue,
- product unavailable,
- fulfillment delay,
- pricing discrepancy,
- suspected fraud,
- customer-requested address change that cannot be fulfilled.

Cancellation reason codes should be stored consistently so that trends can be analyzed.

---

## 7. Company-Initiated Cancellations

The company may cancel an order when fulfillment cannot reasonably proceed.

Possible reasons include:

- insufficient inventory,
- payment authorization failure,
- suspected fraud,
- invalid or undeliverable address,
- product recall,
- system or pricing error,
- inability to fulfill the order within operational requirements.

When the company initiates cancellation, the reason should be recorded and the customer should be notified.

---

## 8. Payment Handling

If an order is cancelled before payment capture, any pending authorization should be released according to payment-provider procedures.

If payment has already been captured, the appropriate refund process should be initiated.

Payment-provider processing time may delay when released or refunded funds become visible to the customer.

---

## 9. Cancellation Timing

Cancellation requests should be processed as quickly as operationally possible.

Customer-requested cancellations should normally be reviewed within 1 business day.

Orders approaching shipment should receive priority because cancellation may become impossible once the carrier accepts the package.

---

## 10. Cancellation Rate Monitoring

Cancellation rate should be monitored across:

- product categories,
- customer segments,
- fulfillment locations,
- payment methods,
- sales channels,
- time periods.

A monthly cancellation rate above 10% should trigger a standard operational review.

A monthly cancellation rate above 15% should trigger a priority review by Order Management and Fulfillment Operations.

These thresholds are internal monitoring triggers and do not determine whether an individual customer's cancellation request is eligible.

---

## 11. Root-Cause Investigation

When cancellation rates exceed an operational threshold, investigation should consider:

- cancellation reason codes,
- inventory availability,
- fulfillment processing time,
- payment failures,
- product availability,
- pricing errors,
- customer-request patterns,
- marketing or promotional events.

A high aggregate cancellation rate should not automatically be attributed to a single cause without supporting evidence.

---

## 12. Escalation Conditions

A cancellation case should be escalated when:

- the order value exceeds $750,
- a customer disputes a denied cancellation,
- repeated cancellation failures occur,
- a system issue prevents eligible cancellations,
- cancellation rates exceed internal monitoring thresholds,
- suspected fraud or abuse is involved.

Escalated cases should be reviewed by Order Management or the appropriate business owner.

---

## 13. Relationship to Shipping and Returns

Cancellation, shipping, and return workflows represent different stages of the order lifecycle.

### Before Shipment

The cancellation process may apply.

### After Shipment

The Shipping and Fulfillment Policy governs shipment handling.

If the customer no longer wants the product, the Return and Refund Policy normally applies after delivery.

### After Delivery

The order should generally be handled through the return process rather than cancellation.

---

## 14. Important Interpretation Notes

The following concepts must remain distinct:

- customer cancellation request,
- company-initiated cancellation,
- failed fulfillment,
- return after delivery.

A customer cancellation request does not guarantee that the order can still be cancelled.

An operational cancellation-rate threshold is a business monitoring trigger and is not an individual customer eligibility rule.

The 15% priority-review threshold should not be interpreted as a target cancellation rate.
