# Shipping and Fulfillment Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy defines the standard process for order fulfillment, shipment handling, delivery expectations, and operational escalation.

The goal is to provide consistent fulfillment practices while minimizing shipping delays, incorrect shipments, and delivery-related customer issues.

---

## 2. Order Processing

Orders normally enter the fulfillment process after successful order confirmation.

An order may remain in processing while the company performs activities such as:

- payment validation,
- inventory allocation,
- warehouse assignment,
- fraud screening,
- packaging preparation.

Orders should generally leave the processing stage within 2 business days.

Orders remaining in processing for more than 3 business days should be reviewed for potential fulfillment issues.

---

## 3. Shipment Expectations

Once an order is ready for fulfillment, it is transferred to the appropriate shipping carrier.

Standard shipments should normally leave the fulfillment facility within 1 business day after fulfillment preparation is complete.

Customers should receive shipment tracking information when the order is handed to the carrier.

---

## 4. Delivery Expectations

Standard domestic orders are expected to arrive within 3 to 7 business days after shipment.

Delivery times may vary because of:

- carrier delays,
- severe weather,
- regional disruptions,
- high-volume seasonal periods,
- incorrect customer address information.

Delivery estimates are not guaranteed unless the customer purchased a service level that explicitly includes a guaranteed delivery date.

---

## 5. Delayed Shipments

An order should be considered operationally delayed when:

- it remains in processing for more than 3 business days, or
- no carrier movement has occurred for more than 2 business days after shipment.

Delayed orders should be reviewed to determine whether intervention is required.

Possible actions include:

- contacting the carrier,
- confirming inventory availability,
- validating shipping information,
- notifying the customer,
- issuing a replacement when appropriate.

---

## 6. Lost Shipments

A shipment may be classified as potentially lost when there has been no tracking movement for 7 consecutive calendar days after the expected carrier movement period.

Before classifying a shipment as lost, Customer Operations should verify:

- tracking history,
- delivery address,
- carrier status,
- customer-reported delivery information.

A confirmed lost shipment may result in:

- replacement,
- full refund,
- store credit,

depending on customer preference and inventory availability.

---

## 7. Incorrect Delivery Address

Customers are responsible for providing accurate delivery information.

If an incorrect address is identified before shipment, the company should attempt to update the order where operationally possible.

If the order has already shipped, address corrections depend on carrier capabilities.

The company is not automatically responsible for delivery failures caused by incorrect customer-provided address information.

---

## 8. Incorrect Item Shipment

When the company ships a product different from the item ordered, the event should be classified as a fulfillment error.

The company should:

- provide return shipping at no cost,
- arrange the correct replacement when available, or
- issue a full refund when replacement is not appropriate.

Incorrect-item incidents should be tracked for fulfillment quality monitoring.

---

## 9. Damaged During Shipping

Damage believed to have occurred during shipping should be documented with available evidence such as:

- photographs,
- packaging condition,
- carrier information,
- customer description.

Confirmed shipping damage should be handled according to the Return and Refund Policy.

The company covers return or replacement shipping for confirmed shipping-related damage.

---

## 10. Fulfillment Performance Monitoring

Fulfillment performance should be monitored using metrics including:

- average processing time,
- average shipping time,
- average delivery time,
- percentage of delayed orders,
- incorrect-item rate,
- lost-shipment rate,
- fulfillment-related cancellation rate.

A monthly delayed-order rate above 8% should trigger an operational review.

A monthly delayed-order rate above 12% should trigger a priority review by Fulfillment Operations.

---

## 11. Escalation Conditions

A fulfillment issue should be escalated when:

- an order remains in processing for more than 5 business days,
- tracking shows no movement for 7 consecutive calendar days,
- repeated fulfillment errors occur for the same warehouse,
- a customer has experienced multiple failed deliveries,
- fulfillment performance exceeds operational thresholds,
- an order value exceeds $750 and a delivery issue remains unresolved.

---

## 12. Relationship to Order Cancellation

An order that has already been shipped should generally not be handled as a standard cancellation.

If the customer no longer wants an order after shipment, the request should normally be handled through the return process after delivery.

Orders may be cancelled before shipment when operationally possible.

Cancellation eligibility may depend on the order's current fulfillment state.

---

## 13. Important Interpretation Notes

The following concepts should not be treated as equivalent:

- processing delay,
- shipping delay,
- delivery delay,
- lost shipment.

A processing delay occurs before shipment.

A shipping or carrier delay occurs after shipment but before delivery.

A lost shipment represents a more severe condition where tracking activity has stopped beyond the defined operational threshold.

Operational review thresholds are internal monitoring rules and do not automatically determine customer compensation.
